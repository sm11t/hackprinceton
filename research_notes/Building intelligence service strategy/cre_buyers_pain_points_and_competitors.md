# CRE Buyer Pain Points, Workflows, and the CRE Data/Analytics Competitive Landscape (as of 2026-11-13; focus Houston, TX)

Method note: 15 tool calls (web searches plus one page fetch). Most numbers come from search-result summaries of trade press (CRE Daily, Bisnow, Commercial Observer, Lee & Associates, CBRE, Cushman), regulator pages, and vendor or review sites. Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** come from low-quality, vendor-marketing, or single secondary sources. Items marked **[OLDER]** are dated before 2026 or have no date. Where I could not find a source, the claim is in Gaps rather than Findings.

## 1. Lenders, servicers and special servicers: maturity wall, distress, CMBS delinquency, collateral monitoring, regulators, and third-party report costs

### Takeaway
The maturity wall is past its peak in dollar terms, but distress is still rising. MBA counts about $875B of commercial/multifamily maturities in 2026 (including $167B office), and Trepp's CMBS delinquency rate hit 8.02% in September 2026, the highest since November 2020. Office CMBS delinquency is about 12%. Regulators want concentration reporting broken out by submarket, property type and borrower, so lenders need more frequent, property-level collateral evidence. Most of that evidence still comes from site inspections and third-party reports that cost thousands of dollars each and take weeks.

### Cited Findings
**Maturity wall**
- MBA: about $875B of commercial/multifamily mortgages mature in 2026, down about 9% from about $957B in 2025. That is about 17% of roughly $5T outstanding. MBA expects annual maturities to keep declining through 2031, and MBA's Reggie Booker called 2026 the point where the market "begins to move past the peak." — [MBA press release, Feb 9 2026](https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/newsroom/news/2026/02/09/17-percent-of-commercial-and-multifamily-mortgage-balances-to-mature-in-2026); [MBA Newslink](https://newslink.mba.org/?p=270033)
- Office: $167B of office mortgages mature in 2026 and another $123B in 2027. Multifamily has about 13% of balances maturing and health care about 15%. — [Briefs.co summary of MBA data](https://www.briefs.co/news/commercial-property-loan-maturities-hit-875-billion-in-2026/)
- S&P Global measures more broadly: about $1.148T maturing in 2026, peaking around $1.257T in 2027. The method differs from MBA's, so the figures are not directly comparable. — [CRE Daily](https://www.credaily.com/briefs/property-debt-maturities-ease-for-cre/)
- MBA forecasts $805B of originations in 2026, up 27% from 2025. — [Multifamily Executive](https://www.multifamilyexecutive.com/debt-equity/multifamily-cre-originations-poised-climb-2026)
- MSCI: the stock of distressed US CRE reached $130.3B at the end of 2025. — cited in [CRE Daily](https://newsletter.credaily.com/p/cre-debt-wave-eases-as-new-loan-activity-rebounds) **[secondary citation of MSCI]**

**CMBS delinquency (Trepp)**
- October 2026 overall: 7.87%, down 15 bps from 8.02% in September. The drop came largely from a $975M showroom loan (High Point / Las Vegas) returning to current. Retail delinquency rose 63 bps, and office and multifamily also rose. — [CRE Daily: "CMBS Delinquency Rate Dips to 7.87% as Retail Jumps 63 bps"](https://www.credaily.com/?p=224934)
- September 2026: 8.02%, the highest since November 2020. Office rose 16 bps to 12.16%, below the all-time high of 12.34% set in January 2026. — [CRE Daily](https://www.credaily.com/?p=224934); see also [Multi-Housing News 2026 CMBS delinquency tracker](https://www.multihousingnews.com/cmbs-delinquency-rates/)
- Earlier 2026 reading: overall 7.85% with office risk rising. — [CRE Daily](https://www.credaily.com/briefs/cmbs-delinquency-holds-at-7-85-as-office-risk-rises/)
- KBRA uses a different measure: 30+ day delinquency on KBRA-rated private-label CMBS was 7.7% in October 2026, down 2 bps. — [KBRA, Oct 2026](https://www.kbra.com/publications/qYPWnQCj/kbra-releases-research-cmbs-loan-performance-trends-october-2026?format=web)
- I did not find the exact October 2026 office rate. It is not in the search snippets; check Trepp's monthly report.

**Regulators (OCC/Fed/FDIC)**
- The OCC issued version 3.0 of its "Concentrations of Credit" Comptroller's Handbook booklet as Bulletin 2026-49. It clarifies correlations vs. concentrations and adds material on securitizations. — [OCC Bulletin 2026-49](https://occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-49.html)
- The 2006 interagency CRE concentration guidance (OCC 2006-46) is still the baseline. Its screening thresholds are CRE loans at ≥300% of capital (with ≥50% growth over 36 months) and construction loans at ≥100% of capital. The 300% figure is a screen, not a cap. — [OCC CRE topic page](https://www.comptrollerofthecurrency.gov/topics/supervision-and-examination/credit/commercial-credit/commercial-real-estate.html) (threshold details from my background knowledge of the 2006 guidance; the page lists the guidance)
- OCC Spring 2026 Semiannual Risk Perspective: office net absorption turned positive in 2H 2025, and banks with more than $250B in assets took additional office charge-offs. — [OCC SRP Spring 2026 PDF](https://www.occ.treas.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/semiannual-risk-perspective/files/pub-semiannual-risk-perspective-spring-2026.pdf); [Crowe summary](https://www.crowe.com/insights/takeaways-from-the-occs-semiannual-risk-perspective)
- The Fed is giving extra scrutiny to community and regional banks with office and multifamily exposure. — [CRE Daily](https://www.credaily.com/briefs/cre-lending-under-fed-scrutiny-amid-office-and-multifamily-risks/)
- **[UNVERIFIED vendor commentary]** Examiners expect concentration reports segmented by submarket, property type and borrower cluster rather than one rolled-up CRE bucket, and they ask how limits changed over the prior 12 months. — [risktemplate blog](https://risktemplate-site.pages.dev/blog/2026-05-08-occ-semiannual-risk-perspective-spring-2026)
- The FDIC and OCC rescinded their 2013 leveraged lending guidance (late 2025). One commentary warns this reduces visibility into risk. — [GlobeSt, Dec 2025](https://www.globest.com/amp/2025/12/09/banks-see-another-post-2009-rule-loosened-leaving-cre-vulnerable/) **[OLDER: Dec 2025]**

**Cost and turnaround of collateral and diligence reports**
- PCA (ASTM E2018; the current version is cited as E2018-24):
  - About $2,500–$8,000 per single building, with a 10–15 business-day turnaround.
  - Another provider prices by size: under 10k SF at $1,800–$3,000; 10–50k SF at $3,000–$5,000; larger or multi-building at $5,000–$8,500+.
  - Other ranges: $3,000–$17,000 (average about $4,000); NEEDDA 2018 guide, about $2,000 to over $20,000.
  - CMBS lenders want full E2018 scope. Fannie and Freddie have their own multifamily scopes. SBA lenders accept limited scope (floor about $1,500).
  - Sources: [US Building Inspections](https://usbuildinginspections.com/property-condition-assessment-report-lender-requirements/); [Trident Inspection](https://tridentinspectiongroup.com/blog/commercial-property-condition-assessments-explained); [AEI/NEEDDA PCA guide (2018)](https://aeiconsultants.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/AEI-NEEDDA-PCA-User-Guide.pdf). **[Vendor marketing; directional only]**
- Phase I ESA: commonly $2,000–$6,500. Sources give $2,000–$10,000, $2,500–$6,500 and $1,800–$10,000. Turnaround is about 3–4 weeks standard, and rush adds 25–50%. — [Fehr Graham](https://www.fehrgraham.com/about-us/blog/phase-i-environmental-site-assessment-cost-fg) **[OLDER: 2023]**; [thepricer.org](https://www.thepricer.org/how-much-does-phase-1-environmental-site-assessment-cost/) **[UNVERIFIED]**; [COR Advisors, Oct 2026](https://www.coradvisors.net/2026/10/phase-1-environmental-site-assessment-cost-by-state-2026.html) **[UNVERIFIED]**
- An institutional deal's standard third-party package has five reports (appraisal, PCA, ESA, zoning, survey). Ordered one after another they would take about 9 weeks, so they are ordered in parallel. — [APERS learn page](https://apers.app/learn/transaction-lifecycle/due-diligence/third-party-reports-appraisal-pca-esa-zoning) **[UNVERIFIED]**
- AI agents are already targeting this workflow. CarbonAI raised $12M in October 2026 (lead: Cowboy Ventures) for CRE lending agents covering underwriting, due diligence and **post-close monitoring**. It reports seven-figure contracted revenue within six months, and Greystone uses it to automate diligence collection. — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/carbonai-12m-cre-lending-agents); [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/carbonai-raises-12-million-led-by-cowboy-ventures-for-ai-powered-commercial-real-estate-lending/)

### Inferences
- Rising delinquency (8%+), office stuck at about 12%, and examiner demands for granular concentration reporting together create a recurring need for property-level evidence between annual inspections. A "remote inspection" product could fill that gap: for example, a Matterport interior plus Vexcel change detection (roof, parking lot occupancy, vacancy signs) plus CoStar occupancy and lease rollover data. It would cost less than a site visit (see Gaps for the visit price) and could run quarterly.
- Special servicers working out office loans need fast answers to "what is this building worth and what does it need": capex, interior condition, how much space is vacant and dark. Each PCA plus appraisal costs about $5k–$15k combined and takes 2–4+ weeks, so a $500–$2,000 "pre-PCA screen" per asset is a plausible price point. This is an inference; no source validates willingness to pay.
- CarbonAI and Cadastral both show investors funding AI for lender workflows in 2026. Their focus is document workflows (T-12s, rent rolls, lease abstraction), not physical or visual building evidence. Physical evidence is the opening for a CoStar + Matterport + Vexcel combination.

### Gaps
- Cost of a lender or servicer **annual site inspection** on the MBA/CREFC standard inspection form. I found no sourced figure. From memory (unverified), it is typically a few hundred dollars per property by third-party inspection firms; verify before use.
- I did not retrieve the MBA Property Inspection Form's frequency requirements (CMBS PSAs typically require annual inspections, more often for watchlisted loans). They are not sourced here.
- Exact Trepp October 2026 office delinquency rate, and any Houston-specific CMBS distress figures.
- Bank CRE concentration levels for Texas/Houston banks.

## 2. Appraisers: timelines, costs, data gaps, AVMs, and the appraiser shortage

### Takeaway
I found little 2026 commercial-appraisal data. Certified general appraiser headcount has been flat (+0.1% from 2020 to 2025) while CRE lending grew. Fees were rising about 5% per year as of 2024, and lenders are pushing for faster, cheaper turn times. I found no evidence that AI has meaningfully cut commercial appraisal turn times by 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Certified general (commercial) appraiser counts rose just 0.1% from 2020 to 2025, while commercial bank CRE loan volume grew 0.6% over the same period. — [Prosight FA](https://www.prosightfa.org/?p=13820)
- The ABA Banking Journal and RMA (Spring 2026) say the industry faces an appraiser shortage. Most appraisers deny it, but longer turn times and public data suggest otherwise. — [ABA Banking Journal, Jun 2026](https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2026/06/effective-strategies-for-managing-an-appraiser-shortage/); [RMA Journal Spring 2026](https://www.rmahq.org/journal-articles/2026/spring-2026/effective-strategies-for-managing-an-appraiser-shortage/)
- LightBox data through Q3 2024 showed CRE appraisal fees up about 5% year over year, with lenders pushing for faster turn times at lower cost. — [LightBox, "Top 5 trends impacting CRE appraisers"](https://lbrestaging.wpengine.com/insight/top-5-trends-impacting-cre-appraisers/) **[OLDER: 2024]**; [LightBox Q2 2025 CRE Snapshot: Appraisal (PDF)](https://go.lightboxre.com/hubfs/Content%20%2B%20Resources/CRE/CRE%20Snapshots/Q2%202025/LightBox%20%7C%20Q2%202025%20CRE%20Snapshot-Appraisal.pdf) (figures not retrieved)
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Typical commercial appraisal fee: $2,000–$6,000. — [generic cost guide](https://new.risingsunartscentre.org/news/commercial-property-appraisal-cost-guide-2026.html)
- Residential context (not CRE): the UAD 3.6 transition raised fees by about $65 on average (Clear Capital), and the national residential turn time is about 7.4 business days. — [NMP](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/uad-36-appraisal-fees-climb-65-early-data-shows-faster-production); [Asteroom](https://blog.asteroom.com/modeling-appraiser-capacity-utilization-and-turn-times-after-uad-3.6)
- Bisnow: appraisers face an "existential crisis as tech creeps in." — [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/national/news/capital-markets/appraisers-face-existential-crisis-as-tech-creeps-in-131410) (date not confirmed)
- CompStak gives brokers and appraisers free access to its comps exchange in return for contributing lease comps. This shows appraisers are data-hungry for comps but price-sensitive. — [MotionCRE Reonomy alternatives](https://motioncre.com/resources/reonomy-alternatives) **[competitor-affiliated source]**

### Inferences
- Appraisers' pain is about verifying the subject property's interior and condition and the inputs from comps (effective rents, concessions). That pain grows when an appraiser can't inspect, or when one appraiser covers a large Texas region. An interior Matterport scan with measured square footage, plus an aerial-verified site area, maps directly onto the subject-property description section of a report.
- Appraisers individually have low ability to pay. The better buyer is the lender's appraisal management or review desk, or the AMC, which orders thousands of reports.

### Gaps
- Commercial appraisal turnaround in 2026: from memory (unverified), typically 2–4 weeks and longer for complex assets.
- Commercial appraisal fees by property type in Houston.
- Adoption of CRE AVMs by bank regulators (the interagency AVM quality-control rule finalized in 2024 targets residential mortgage AVMs; not verified here).

## 3. Brokers (leasing and investment sales): prospecting, comps, marketing, and CoStar frustrations

### Takeaway
Brokers' main complaints are CoStar's price and terms: about $500–$560 per seat per month in recent public contracts, about $15k median annual contracts, CPI-linked automatic increases, a 60-day auto-renew notice window, a requirement that each broker hold a seat, and a ban on feeding the data into third-party AI. Two 2026 antitrust class actions plus Crexi's own suit make CoStar's dominance (an alleged ~80% share) a live issue. Cheaper alternatives cost about $2.4k–$5k per seat per year (Crexi Intelligence, Reonomy).

### Cited Findings
**CoStar seat pricing**
- Recent public-contract seat prices (per user per month):
  - Chicago DPD: $387 in 2021, $502 in 2024, $553 in 2025. That is +43% over 2021–2025 and about +10% in year five.
  - Irvine (Sep 2024): about $557 per user.
  - San Diego (Dec 2025): about $507 per user (16 users).
  - Source: [MarketIntelligenceTools CoStar pricing report, checked Nov 9 2026](https://marketintelligencetools.com/reports/costar-pricing/)
- Vendr reports a median CoStar contract of $15,360 per year (range $5,412–$22,092), but on only 3 deals. — [same](https://marketintelligencetools.com/reports/costar-pricing/)

**CoStar contract terms**
- CoStar's 2026 terms (effective Oct 9 2026):
  - Annual CPI increases are allowed.
  - Auto-renewal applies unless the customer gives written notice 60 days before the term ends.
  - The right to exit over a renewal increase now applies only to increases above CPI (the 2018 terms allowed exit over any increase).
  - Fees can be raised for unlisted users.
  - Sharing data with **third-party AI services** is prohibited.
  - Licensees must delete data within 10 days of termination.
  - Source: [same](https://marketintelligencetools.com/reports/costar-pricing/)
- CoStar's 2025 10-K attributes part of a $103M (9%) product revenue increase to "inflation-based price increases." — [same](https://marketintelligencetools.com/reports/costar-pricing/)
- Trustpilot reviews (Nov 2024, June 2026) complain about auto-renewal lock-ins and year-two price jumps (for example, "$6,500+ a year"). — [same](https://marketintelligencetools.com/reports/costar-pricing/) **[anecdotal]**

**Antitrust litigation**
- April 2026 class action (Grand & Co., E.D. Va.): a boutique brokerage alleges CoStar monopolized online CRE listings with about 80% share. The suit claims every broker at a firm needs an individual subscription at about $300–$1,000 per month. CoStar denies the claims and cites Crexi as competition. — [Berger Montague complaint PDF](https://bergermontague.com/wp-content/uploads/2026-04-15-CoStar-dkt-1-Pltf-Complaint.pdf); [DiCello Levitt complaint PDF](https://dicellolevitt.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Antitrust-Class-Action-Complaint-Against-CoStar-Group.pdf); [CRE Daily](https://www.credaily.com/?p=217210)
- June 2026 class action (N.D. Ill.): a commercial tenant sues CoStar, CBRE, JLL, Cushman & Wakefield, Colliers and Newmark. It alleges a rent price-fixing hub-and-spoke scheme across 49 metros, with rents rising 0.3%–0.8% per SF for each 1-point gain in CoStar share. These are allegations. — [The Real Deal, Jun 17 2026](https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/06/17/costar-hit-with-lawsuit-alleging-price-fixing-for-cre-rents/); [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/commercial-real-estate/costar-5-major-brokerages-hit-with-class-action-lawsuit-alleging-price-fixing-conspiracy-135043); [Facilities Dive](https://www.facilitiesdive.com/news/major-commercial-real-estate-brokers-costar-antitrust-lawsuit/823286/)
- Crexi is running its own antitrust suit against CoStar. — [Propmodo](https://propmodo.com/?p=180688)

**Activist pressure**
- Third Point is pressuring CoStar over its multibillion-dollar Homes.com spending. Homes.com pricing was cut more than 30% in 2025, and CoStar is pushing prices up again in 2026. This bears on CoStar's incentive to squeeze CRE seat revenue. — [nowbam](https://nowbam.com/investor-pressures-costar-over-homes-com-losses-in-letter-to-board/) **[secondary]**

**Alternatives**
- Crexi: the marketplace is free to search. Crexi Intelligence costs about $2,388 per user per year (about $199/month annual), or $269–$299/month on other plans; sources conflict. — [TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/products/crexi/pricing); [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/crm/crexi-profile/)

### Inferences
- The broker pain a "building intelligence" product can address is **marketing and pitch material**: interior tours, aerial context, and stacking plans and comps in one shareable page for a listing pitch or a tenant-rep tour. CoStar's terms bar feeding its data to third-party AI. A product built on CoStar records therefore needs a CoStar licensing or partnership path, or must be positioned as a CoStar-sanctioned add-on. This is a **critical business-model risk** for the hackathon concept.
- Brokers are price-sensitive and already resent per-seat fees. Per-listing or per-report pricing (for example, $99–$499 per building package) likely fits better than another seat license.

### Gaps
- Systematic data on CoStar data-error complaints from brokers (for example, Reddit r/CommercialRealEstate threads). Not retrieved here.
- Broker time spent on comps and prospecting per deal.
- Houston-specific broker tooling (HAR's commercial MLS usage). Not researched.

## 4. Investors / acquisitions and asset managers: diligence timelines and costs, physical diligence, capex surprises, Houston insurance, portfolio monitoring

### Takeaway
Physical diligence is a standard bundle of five third-party reports that takes weeks and costs about $5k–$25k+ per asset, before legal and survey costs. In Houston, insurance (wind, flood, and changes to FEMA maps) is a major underwriting variable, but reliable 2026 commercial premium data is scarce and opaque. That scarcity is itself a pain point. AI underwriting startups are well funded but focused on documents.

### Cited Findings
**Diligence process and AI startups**
- Five standard third-party reports (appraisal, PCA, ESA, zoning, survey) would take about 9 weeks if ordered one after another, so they are ordered in parallel. — [APERS](https://apers.app/learn/transaction-lifecycle/due-diligence/third-party-reports-appraisal-pca-esa-zoning) **[UNVERIFIED]**
- PCA costs $2.5k–$8.5k+ and Phase I costs $2k–$6.5k; see section 1 for sources.
- Cadastral raised $9.5M in February 2026 from Navitas, JLL Spark, AvalonBay, Equity Residential and 1Sharpe. Its "AI analyst in a box" covers T-12 analysis, lease abstraction, loan compliance, acquisitions diligence and underwriting. — [Mann Report](https://www.mannpublications.com/mannreport/2026/02/05/cadastral-raises-9-5m/); [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/cadastral-9-5-million/)
- Diald AI raised $3.75M in December 2025 for AI diligence and underwriting in the US and Japan. It claims to turn "weeks of research into hours." — [Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2025/12/diald-ai-early-stage-funding/) **[OLDER: Dec 2025]**
- PlexAI (Israel, pre-seed, founded 2025) is building CRE underwriting and diligence agents. — [startupim](https://startupim.com/company/plexai) **[UNVERIFIED]**

**Houston insurance and flood risk**
- TWIA covers wind in designated coastal territory. The TWIA board recommended no rate change for 2026 residential and commercial policies (July 2025). TWIA published a June 30 2026 commercial rate-level review, but its rate figure was not retrieved. — [Insurance Journal, Jul 2025](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southcentral/2025/07/15/831577.htm); [TWIA 2026 Commercial Memo](https://www.twia.org/wp-content/uploads/Commercial-Memo-2026.pdf). Note: most of Harris County is inland of the TWIA catastrophe area. That is from my background knowledge, not verified here.
- Flood:
  - Since FEMA's Risk Rating 2.0 launched (2021), average Texas flood premiums rose about 35% while the number of covered buildings fell about 30%. — [Carrier Management, May 2025](https://www.carriermanagement.com/news/2025/05/20/275462.htm) **[OLDER]**
  - Texas leads the nation in flood insurance cancellations as costs rise (June 2026). — [KPRC Click2Houston](https://www.click2houston.com/news/local/2026/06/03/texas-leads-nation-in-flood-insurance-cancellations-as-costs-rise-ahead-of-hurricane-season/)
  - FEMA's proposed 2026 Harris County flood map update would move more properties into high-risk zones, which triggers mandatory flood insurance for federally backed loans. — [Neptune Flood analysis](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/564726/neptune-flood-research-group-analyzes-impact-of-fema-s-proposed-2026-harris-county-texas-flood-map-update) **[insurer source; promotional]**
- Commercial exposure (older):
  - "Almost all" Houston commercial property carries some flood financial risk (CoreLogic executive). — [Bisnow, undated](https://www.bisnow.com/news/houston/commercial-real-estate/flood-insurance-on-the-rise-insurers-worry-of-future-damage-risk-100052) **[OLDER]**
  - A 2021 study found 34.1% of Harris County commercial properties at risk of being inoperable from flooding.
  - One property's flood rate rose 40% in a single year, and many faced 15%+ increases.
  - Post-Harvey, carriers cut flood limits, for example from $10M to $2.5M.
  - NFIP limits are too low for most commercial losses, so cover goes to the private market.
  - Source for all four: [Bisnow, undated](https://www.bisnow.com/news/houston/commercial-real-estate/flood-insurance-on-the-rise-insurers-worry-of-future-damage-risk-100052) **[OLDER]**
- More than 65,000 new residential, commercial and industrial developments were built in flood hazard zones across the five largest Houston-area counties over eight years. — [Houston Chronicle investigation](https://preview-prod.w.houstonchronicle.com/news/investigations/article/takeaways-houston-floodplain-building-boom-20403775.php) (date not confirmed)
- Example NFIP premium: $2,841 per year for a slab commercial property in Zone X in Houston (May 2026 example). — [Flood Insurance Guru](https://www.floodinsuranceguru.com/the-flood-insurance-guru-blog/understanding-flood-insurance-costs-in-houston-2026-insights) **[single example; broker blog]**
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Houston multifamily insurance costs more than $1,200 per unit per year, about double low-risk markets. — [texasmultifamilyquotes](https://texasmultifamilyquotes.pages.dev/multifamily/houston) (poor-quality source; do not cite as fact)

### Inferences
- Houston's specific opening is a per-building **physical-risk underwriting pack**: Vexcel aerial and oblique imagery, plus roof condition from aerial, plus first-floor elevation and slab-vs-raised evidence from Matterport and oblique imagery, plus the floodplain layer. This feeds insurance quotes (elevation certificates, wind mitigation features), PCA scoping and capex budgets. Lenders and investors cannot get these quickly today.
- Asset managers' recurring pain is portfolio-level monitoring of physical condition and post-storm damage (after Beryl in 2024 and Harvey in 2017). A post-event aerial-diff alert across a portfolio is a clear, recurring, sellable workflow.

### Gaps
- Typical CRE acquisition diligence period (commonly 30–60 days in PSAs). Not sourced here.
- Total third-party report spend per deal, and capex-surprise frequency or magnitude statistics.
- 2026 Houston commercial property insurance premium figures (rate per $100 of TIV) for office, industrial and multifamily. I found no reliable source.
- Final adoption status of the Harris County FEMA map update.

## 5. Property/facilities managers and corporate occupiers: utilization, return to office, lease administration, maintenance, ESG

### Takeaway
Houston office attendance is above the national average: the Kastle 10-city average was 56.3% versus 57.9% in Houston (week of Sept 16 2026). Occupiers are consolidating into Class A, which keeps vacancy at about 24–26%. Research coverage here is thin; most claims about occupier workflows are unsourced.

### Cited Findings
- Kastle Back to Work Barometer: Houston average weekly office occupancy was 57.9% for the week ending September 16 2026, versus the 10-city average of 56.3%. — [Lee & Associates Greater Houston Office Q3 2026](https://www.lee-associates.com/houston/2026/10/19/greater-houston-office-market-report-q3-2026/)
- An older Kastle reading for Houston: a peak-day figure of 74.8% in 2025. — referenced in the same search; [InnovationMap Kastle tag](https://houston.innovationmap.com/tag/kastle-systems) **[OLDER]**
- Houston tenants favor Class A, and Q3 2026 leasing fell 17.9% to 2.6M SF (Newmark). — [CRE Daily](https://www.credaily.com/?p=224274)
- Houston's office reset is driving a demolition and conversion boom. — [CRE Daily](https://www.credaily.com/?p=219743)
- Cherre (a real estate data and connectivity platform used by asset and property managers) was reportedly acquired by RealPage on July 14 2026. — [bestcre.com Cherre review](https://bestcre.com/cherre-review-cre-ai/) **[UNVERIFIED single source]**

### Inferences
- For occupiers, the relevant uses are space planning and "test fits" from Matterport scans, and evidence for lease administration (measured RSF vs. leased RSF disputes). This is a weaker fit with CoStar records than the lender and investor use cases.
- Property managers in Houston would value storm-readiness documentation: a baseline Matterport scan plus pre- and post-storm aerials for insurance claims. This is plausible but not validated here.

### Gaps
- Lease administration pain (CAM reconciliation errors, critical date misses), energy and ESG mandates for Houston. Texas has no BPS-type benchmarking law equivalent to NYC's LL97; that is from background knowledge, not verified. Facility condition assessment cost and workflow. ## 6. Competitors in CRE data/analytics and where each stops

### Takeaway
Most incumbents sell **records** (transactions, leases, ownership, debt, foot traffic) or **workflow software** (deal pipeline, leasing). Almost none sell **verified current physical condition**: what the building looks like inside today, roof and site condition, and measured space. AI-native startups in 2025–2026 automate documents (T-12s, rent rolls, leases) rather than physical evidence. Pricing is mostly sales-quote only. Published figures: CoStar about $500–$560 per seat per month; Reonomy about $4.8k per seat per year; Crexi Intelligence about $2.4k per seat per year.

### Cited Findings (competitor-by-competitor)
- **CoStar Group (CoStar Suite, LoopNet; owns Matterport since its 2025 acquisition):**
  - About $502–$557 per seat per month in 2024–2025 public contracts.
  - Median annual contract about $15,360 (Vendr, n=3).
  - Terms ban sharing with third-party AI.
  - Faces two 2026 antitrust class actions plus Crexi's suit.
  - Source: [MarketIntelligenceTools](https://marketintelligencetools.com/reports/costar-pricing/); [The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/national/2026/06/17/costar-hit-with-lawsuit-alleging-price-fixing-for-cre-rents/)

- **Crexi:** free marketplace. Crexi Intelligence about $2,388 per user per year (or $269–$299/month). It is suing CoStar on antitrust grounds. Where it stops: listings and ownership data, with no interior or condition data. — [TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/products/crexi/pricing); [Propmodo](https://propmodo.com/?p=180688)

### Inferences
- **White space:** a "physical truth layer" per building that is current, verified, and visual. It would link the records layer (CoStar leases and availability) to observed reality: Matterport interiors showing built-out vs. shell and dark space, and Vexcel aerials showing roof, parking utilization and site changes. The buyers that most need it in 2026 are special servicers and lenders monitoring office collateral, insurers and investors underwriting Houston wind and flood risk, and acquisitions teams scoping PCAs.
- **Competitive risk:** CoStar owns Matterport (verify), and CoStar's terms ban third-party AI use of its data. The most realistic commercial path is a CoStar-internal product or partnership, or a product that uses public records plus customer-supplied data in place of CoStar records.
- **Pricing benchmarks:** data seats cost about $2.4k–$15k+ per year, and third-party reports cost about $2k–$10k each. A per-building monitoring subscription (for example, $50–$300 per building per month for lender portfolios) or a per-report screen ($500–$2,000) sits between the two. These are inferred figures and are not validated by sources.

## 7. Houston specifics: office, industrial, data centers, flood

### Takeaway
Houston office vacancy is about 24–26% on full-market measures and flat in 2026, with tenants moving to Class A and older stock being demolished. Industrial is strong: record rents of $8.16 per SF, 14.6M SF of Q3 leasing, and 6.3% vacancy, partly driven by data-center-related demand. Flood and insurance risk sits over everything.

### Cited Findings
**Office, Q3 2026** (measures differ, so the figures are not directly comparable)
- Newmark: 25.6% vacancy, flat from Q2 and +30 bps year over year. Leasing fell 17.9% to 2.6M SF, and absorption was −31,374 SF. — [CRE Daily](https://www.credaily.com/?p=224274)
- CBRE: 23.9% vacancy; CBD 25.5%; Katy Freeway 10.4%. — [CBRE Houston Office Figures Q3 2026](https://www.cbre.com/insights/figures/houston-office-figures-q3-2026)
- Cushman & Wakefield: 24.6% vacancy (+10 bps QoQ, +70 bps YoY). — [C&W Houston Office MarketBeat](https://www.cushmanwakefield.com/en/united-states/insights/us-marketbeats/houston-marketbeats/office)
- Lee & Associates: 19.4% direct vacancy; absorption −39,738 SF; YTD absorption about +50,900 SF. — [Lee & Associates Q3 2026](https://www.lee-associates.com/houston/2026/10/19/greater-houston-office-market-report-q3-2026/)
- Savills: total availability 26.2% (−60 bps YoY); The Woodlands is lowest at 14.4%. — [Savills](https://www.savills.us/research_articles/256536/394934-0)

**Flood**
- See section 4: 34.1% of Harris County commercial properties at flood-inoperability risk (2021 study), the FEMA 2026 map update, and Texas leading the nation in flood-policy cancellations.

### Inferences
- **Houston office:** the use case is distress triage. Servicers and banks need to know which Class B/C buildings are physically obsolete (demolition or conversion candidates) and which are re-leasable. Interiors plus aerials plus CoStar vacancy history answer that directly.
- **Houston industrial and data centers:** the use case is site selection and verification, for example yard and parking area, roof condition for solar and power gear, and change detection on land being built. Vexcel aerials fit well.
